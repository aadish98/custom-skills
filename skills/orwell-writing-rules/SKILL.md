# Orwell's writing rules

Source: George Orwell, "[Politics and the English Language](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/)" (1946). His target was writing that conceals meaning: dead metaphors that save the writer from thinking, long words that inflate simple claims, passives that hide who acted, and jargon that signals membership instead of conveying content.

Apply these to every sentence, and let rule 6 override the rest:

1. Never use a metaphor, simile, or other figure of speech which you are used to seeing in print.
2. Never use a long word where a short one will do.
3. If it is possible to cut a word out, always cut it out.
4. Never use the passive where you can use the active.
5. Never use a foreign phrase, a scientific word, or a jargon word if you can think of an everyday English equivalent.
6. Break any of these rules sooner than say anything outright barbarous.

## Rule 6 governs the other five

Mechanical obedience to rules 1–5 produces its own kind of bad prose. A rule yields whenever following it would make a sentence worse. Read the result aloud; if it sounds stilted or unclear, the rule was applied too rigidly.

## Rule 5 and precise terminology

Rule 5 targets imprecise jargon, not precise terminology. Keep the exact technical term when it carries meaning an everyday word would lose, and define it on first use. Cut the words that obscure the point, not the substance itself.

## Rule 4 and structure

Passive voice is the default to avoid, but structure sometimes overrides it — see the `scientific-writing-structure` skill for when the passive correctly keeps the right topic in front of the reader.

## Checklist

- [ ] Remove clichés and stock figures of speech (rule 1)
- [ ] Swap long or Latinate words for everyday ones, keeping precise technical terms (rules 2 and 5)
- [ ] Cut every word that carries no weight (rule 3)
- [ ] Prefer the active voice, unless structure argues otherwise (rule 4)
- [ ] Reread: break any rule above rather than write something barbarous or unclear (rule 6)
